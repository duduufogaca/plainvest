How dollar-cost averaging works

Instead of trying to guess the perfect moment to invest, you invest the same amount on a fixed schedule. When prices are high, your fixed amount buys fewer units; when prices drop, it buys more. Over time this averages out your cost and removes the emotional pressure of "is now the right time?"

💡 DCA's real power isn't math — it's behaviour. By automating a fixed amount, you keep investing through both rising and falling markets, which is exactly when most people freeze.

Why beginners love DCA

  • No need to time the market or watch charts daily.
  • Works perfectly with regular income — invest as you earn.
  • Turns investing into a calm, automatic habit instead of a gamble.

Frequently asked questions

What is dollar-cost averaging (DCA)?

Investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule regardless of price. High prices buy fewer units, low prices buy more — averaging your cost and removing market-timing pressure.

Is DCA better than a lump sum?

Both have merits. Lump-sum has historically come out ahead on average because money is invested longer, but DCA reduces regret risk and suits investing from regular income. The best plan is the one you stick to.

How often should I invest?

Weekly, fortnightly or monthly all work. Consistency and automation matter more than frequency — set it and avoid reacting to short-term noise.
