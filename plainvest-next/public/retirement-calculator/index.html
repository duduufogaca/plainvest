How retirement projections work

Three things shape your retirement: how much you've saved, how much you add each month, and how many years your money has to compound. Because growth builds on growth, the years you start earliest are worth the most — even small contributions early can outpace large ones late.

💡 The income guideline: at retirement, a portfolio can sustainably provide roughly 4% per year. So a $1,000,000 balance could support about $40,000 a year, adjusted for inflation.

What moves the number most

  • Time — starting 10 years earlier can dramatically change the outcome.
  • Consistency — automated monthly contributions compound powerfully.
  • Costs & inflation — keeping fees low and beating inflation protects your real result.

Frequently asked questions

How does a retirement calculator work?

It projects how current savings plus ongoing contributions could grow by your chosen retirement age, using an assumed average annual return compounded over the years remaining.

How much do I need to retire?

A common guideline is roughly 25× your annual expenses, so a portfolio can provide income at about a 4% withdrawal rate. Your real figure depends on lifestyle, taxes and time horizon.

What return should I assume?

Diversified long-term portfolios have historically averaged ~7–8% per year before inflation. These are educational assumptions — real returns vary, so revisit your plan regularly.
