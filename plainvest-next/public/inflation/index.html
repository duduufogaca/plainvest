How inflation affects your money

Inflation means prices rise over time, so each dollar buys a little less. If your money sits in a low-interest account while prices climb, its real value — what it can actually buy — shrinks quietly every year, even though the number on your statement looks the same.

💡 At ~3% inflation, money loses roughly half its purchasing power in about 23 years. The balance doesn't drop — what it can buy does.

Why this matters

  • Cash feels safe, but idle cash steadily loses value to inflation.
  • Inflation is the benchmark your money needs to beat to truly grow.
  • Historically, diversified investing has grown far faster than inflation over long periods.

Frequently asked questions

What does an inflation calculator show?

It shows how the real value (purchasing power) of money changes over time as prices rise. A sum that buys a lot today buys less in the future if it isn't growing.

What is a normal inflation rate?

Long-term inflation often averages around 2–3% per year, though it can spike higher in some periods. We use ~3% as an educational default.

How do I protect my money from inflation?

Historically, investing in diversified growth assets such as index funds has outpaced inflation over long periods, while idle cash loses value. A long-term plan and education are the starting point.
