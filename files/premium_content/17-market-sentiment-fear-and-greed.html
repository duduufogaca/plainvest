How to read this index

What it measures

Market sentiment — whether investors are acting out of panic (fear) or overconfidence (greed). Built from volatility, momentum, options activity, and other signals. It does not measure the value of an asset. It measures the emotional state of the crowd around it.

How to use it

It is a contrarian indicator, not a timing tool. Extreme fear has historically appeared near market bottoms; extreme greed near tops. The key word is "historically" — past patterns do not guarantee future results. Use it as context, not instruction.

Crypto vs stocks

Both indices often move together, especially during broad risk-off events like rate hikes or macro uncertainty. They can diverge sharply during crypto-specific events — a Bitcoin halving, a major exchange collapse, or a regulatory ruling. A large gap between the two is worth researching before acting.

Warren Buffett's rule

"Be fearful when others are greedy and greedy when others are fearful." The index makes that principle visible. It does not tell you when to act — that is what your written investment plan is for. The index removes some of the guesswork about where the crowd currently stands.

The most important thing to understand: a high fear reading does not guarantee prices will recover. A high greed reading does not guarantee prices will fall. These are probability-weighted observations from market history, not laws. Your plan — not the gauge — determines what you do.

What each index actually measures

CNN Fear & Greed — US Stock Market (7 indicators)

Stock price momentum

Compares the S&P 500 to its 125-day moving average. When the index is trading well above its average, momentum is bullish (greed). When it is below, momentum is bearish (fear).

Stock price strength

Measures the ratio of 52-week highs vs lows on the NYSE. More new highs than lows signals greed; the reverse signals fear.

Stock price breadth

Uses the McClellan Volume Summation Index — whether advancing stocks are supported by high trading volume (greed) or declining stocks are (fear).

Put and call options ratio

Compares the volume of put options (bets on price falls) to call options (bets on price rises). More puts than usual signals fear; more calls signals greed.

Junk bond demand

Looks at the yield spread between investment-grade and junk bonds. When investors chase yield in risky bonds (spread narrows), it signals greed. Wide spreads signal fear.

Market volatility (VIX)

The CBOE Volatility Index measures implied volatility in the S&P 500 options market. High VIX = fear; low VIX = complacency (greed).

The seventh indicator is safe-haven demand — the relative performance of stocks vs Treasury bonds over 20 days. When investors prefer the safety of bonds, it signals fear. When stocks outperform strongly, it signals greed.

Alternative.me — Crypto Market (5 weighted factors)

Volatility — 25%

Compares current Bitcoin price volatility against its 30-day and 90-day averages. Unusual volatility spikes, especially to the downside, are treated as a sign of fear in the market.

Market momentum and volume — 25%

Compares current Bitcoin trading volume against recent averages. High buying volume in a rising market signals greed. Low volume or high sell volume signals fear.

Social media sentiment — 15%

Analyses the volume and sentiment of Bitcoin-related content on social platforms. Very high positive engagement and hashtag activity is treated as a signal of greed.

Bitcoin dominance — 10%

Tracks Bitcoin's share of total crypto market cap. Rising Bitcoin dominance can signal fear (investors retreating from altcoins to the relative safety of Bitcoin). Falling dominance can signal risk appetite.

The fifth factor is Google Trends (10%) — the search volume for Bitcoin-related terms and the ratio of positive to negative search queries. Surges in searches like "Bitcoin crash" signal fear; surges in "Bitcoin price prediction" or "how to buy Bitcoin" signal greed.

What the index does not tell you

It does not predict timing

Markets can remain in Extreme Fear for months (March 2020, November 2022) or in Extreme Greed for extended periods. A reading alone is not a reliable entry or exit trigger. Position sizing and patience matter more than the exact number.

It does not measure value

A 40 reading does not mean an asset is cheap. A 75 reading does not mean it is expensive. The index measures crowd psychology, not intrinsic value. Combine it with price history and your own research.

It does not account for fundamentals

Bitcoin at Extreme Fear in a post-halving year with strong on-chain metrics is different from Bitcoin at Extreme Fear during a project collapse or regulatory ban. Context always matters more than the score.

It does not replace a plan

If your plan says "add $300/month regardless of sentiment," the index should not change that. It can be useful for sizing discretionary decisions or managing emotional reactions, but it is not a strategy on its own.
