8 things to compare

1. Total cost

Brokerage, FX spread, account fees, inactivity fees, and fund management fees all matter. A cheap trade can still be expensive if the FX spread is high.

2. Product access

Can you buy the ETFs, shares, or markets you actually want? Check ASX, US shares, managed funds, and fractional investing rules.

3. Ownership model

Understand CHESS sponsorship, custodian models, HINs, and what proof of ownership or statements you receive.

4. Tax reporting

Check annual statements, dividend reports, trade confirmations, CSV exports, and whether your accountant can use them easily.

5. Automation

If your plan is monthly DCA, auto-invest features and recurring deposits can matter more than fancy charts.

6. Security

Look for two-factor authentication, withdrawal controls, device management, and support if your account is compromised.

7. Beginner clarity

The best platform for a beginner is often the one they can use safely without accidentally buying the wrong product.

8. Support

Check how support works before you need it. Email-only support may be fine, but know the response expectations.

How to avoid choosing the wrong platform

  1. Write your investing plan first. Platform choice should follow your goal, not create it.
  2. Choose your first asset type. ASX ETF, US ETF, individual shares, crypto, or managed fund?
  3. Compare the cost of your actual behaviour. Monthly $200 DCA and occasional $5,000 buys have different fee sensitivity.
  4. Open with a small test amount. Learn deposits, order placement, confirmations, and withdrawals before moving larger amounts.
  5. Keep tax exports from day one. The best-looking app can become annoying if reporting is messy.

Plainvest platform rule

A platform is a tool, not a strategy. Do not pick one because a creator promoted it, because the app looks exciting, or because it offers the most products. Pick the platform that makes your beginner plan easier to follow, safer to record, and calmer to repeat.
