CHESS vs custodian: what ownership model means in Australia

This is one of the most misunderstood differences between Australian platforms. It affects what happens to your shares if the broker has a problem.

CHESS sponsored (direct ownership)

  • You receive a personal HIN (Holder Identification Number)
  • Your name is on the ASX register directly
  • Shares belong to you regardless of broker status
  • Can transfer HIN to a different broker if needed
  • Platforms: CommSec, SelfWealth, Pearler, Stake (paid upgrade)

Custodian model (beneficial ownership)

  • Broker holds shares on your behalf in a pooled account
  • You are the beneficial owner but not on the ASX register
  • If broker faces insolvency, access may be temporarily frozen
  • Generally fine for established regulated platforms — but understand the model
  • Platforms: CommSec Pocket, Vanguard Personal, BetaShares Direct
Practical reality: custodian platforms are ASIC-regulated and generally safe for most beginner investors. But CHESS gives you direct ownership that doesn't depend on broker solvency. For buy-and-hold ETF investing with amounts that matter to you, CHESS is worth the slightly higher brokerage at larger amounts.

US account types: which wrapper to use first

Roth IRA

After-tax contributions. Growth and withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. 2024 limit: $7,000/year ($8,000 if over 50). Use this before a taxable brokerage account if you qualify.

Traditional IRA

Pre-tax contributions may be deductible. Growth is tax-deferred; withdrawals taxed in retirement. Same contribution limits as Roth. Compare your current vs expected retirement tax rate.

401(k) / Employer plan

Employer-sponsored retirement account. Always contribute at least enough to get the full employer match — that's an instant 50–100% return before any market growth.

Taxable brokerage

No contribution limits. No tax advantages. Capital gains tax applies on profits. Use this after maximising tax-advantaged options, or for goals before retirement age.

Key concepts that differ in Brazil

FGC guarantee

The Fundo Garantidor de Créditos protects up to R$250,000 per CPF per institution for CDB, LCI, LCA, and savings deposits. Spread across institutions if balances exceed this threshold.

Income tax on fixed income

CDB and Tesouro Prefixado/IPCA+ are subject to progressive income tax (22.5% under 6 months, down to 15% over 2 years). LCI and LCA are income tax exempt for individuals — compare net returns.

Come-cotas (funds)

Brazilian investment funds (fundos) are subject to come-cotas — a biannual tax collection on May 31 and November 30 that reduces your units even if you haven't sold. ETFs on B3 are not subject to come-cotas.

B3 vs Tesouro Direto

B3 is Brazil's stock exchange (stocks, ETFs, FIIs). Tesouro Direto is a separate government bond programme. Both are accessed through the same corretora, but they are different products with different risks.

8 things to compare regardless of country

1. Total cost

Brokerage, FX spread, account fees, inactivity fees, and fund management fees all matter. A cheap trade can still be expensive if the FX spread is high.

2. Product access

Can you buy the ETFs, shares, or markets you actually want? Check local exchange access, international products, managed funds, and fractional investing rules.

3. Ownership model

CHESS (AU), SIPC insurance (US), FGC coverage (BR). Understand what protection exists and how your assets are held.

4. Tax reporting

Annual statements, dividend and distribution reports, trade confirmations, and CSV exports. Check whether your accountant in your country can use the format provided.

5. Automation

If your plan is monthly DCA, auto-invest features and recurring deposits can matter more than fancy charts.

6. Security

Look for two-factor authentication, withdrawal controls, device management, and responsive support if your account is compromised.

7. Beginner clarity

The best platform for a beginner is often the one they can use safely without accidentally buying the wrong product or market.

8. Support quality

Check how support works before you need it. Know if support is in your language, how long responses take, and what happens during platform outages.

How to avoid choosing the wrong platform

  1. Write your investing plan first. Platform choice should follow your goal, not create it.
  2. Choose your first asset type and country. ASX ETF, US ETF, Brazilian fixed income, or international stocks?
  3. Compare the cost of your actual behaviour. Monthly small DCA and occasional larger buys have different fee sensitivity.
  4. Open with a small test amount. Learn deposits, order placement, confirmations, and withdrawals before moving larger amounts.
  5. Check your country's tax reporting needs from day one. The best-looking app can become annoying if reporting is messy at tax time.

Plainvest platform rule

A platform is a tool, not a strategy. Do not pick one because a creator promoted it, because the app looks exciting, or because it offers the most products. Pick the platform that makes your beginner plan easier to follow, safer to record, and calmer to repeat.
