CHESS vs custodian: the ownership model that matters

This is one of the most important — and most misunderstood — differences between Australian platforms. Understanding it before you open an account prevents regret later.

✅ CHESS sponsored — direct ownership

  • You receive a personal HIN (Holder Identification Number)
  • Your name appears directly on the ASX register
  • Shares legally belong to you regardless of broker status
  • HIN can be transferred to any other CHESS-sponsored broker without selling
  • Annual holding statements issued directly by the registry (Computershare, Link)
  • Platforms: CommSec, SelfWealth, Pearler, Stake (paid upgrade)

⚠️ Custodian model — beneficial ownership

  • Platform holds assets in a pooled account on your behalf
  • You are the beneficial owner but do not appear on the ASX register
  • If the broker faces insolvency, assets may be frozen temporarily during resolution
  • Cannot transfer holdings to a CHESS broker without selling first
  • Generally fine for ASIC-regulated, established platforms — but understand the structure
  • Platforms: CommSec Pocket, Vanguard Personal, BetaShares Direct, Moomoo, IBKR
Practical reality for beginners: custodian platforms from established, ASIC-regulated issuers are safe for most investors. The key risk is operational (access delays during platform issues) rather than loss of assets. For long-term ETF portfolios where you want full control, portability, and the strongest legal ownership position, CHESS is the standard to target as your portfolio grows.

The United States has the most competitive retail brokerage market in the world. Commission-free trading became the standard in 2019 when Schwab cut commissions to zero, forcing every major broker to follow within days. The result: US investors now pay nothing to buy or sell stocks and ETFs at the major platforms. The choice between US platforms therefore comes down to research quality, account types, fractional shares, platform depth, and the specific funds available.

VTI (US total market, 0.03%), VOO (S&P 500, 0.03%), VXUS (international, 0.07%), BND (US bonds, 0.03%), VT (global all-world, 0.07%). These are available at every US brokerage and can be transferred between platforms.

SCHB (US total market, 0.03%), SCHX (US large cap, 0.03%), SCHY (international dividend, 0.14%), SCHD (US dividend, 0.06%). Very competitive costs; available at Schwab and most other brokers.

iShares (BlackRock) ETFs

IVV (S&P 500, 0.03%), IEFA (international developed, 0.07%), AGG (US bonds, 0.03%), ITOT (US total market, 0.03%). Widely available, extremely liquid, frequently used by institutional investors.

US account types: which wrapper to use and in what order

1. 401(k) with employer match — always first

If your employer offers a 401(k) match, contribute enough to receive the full match before anything else. An employer matching 50% of your contribution up to 6% of salary is an instant 50% return before any market gain. This is the highest-return "investment" available to most US workers.

2. Roth IRA — after getting full match

Contributions are after-tax, but growth and qualifying withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free. 2024 contribution limit: $7,000/year ($8,000 if aged 50+). Open at Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard. Invest in low-cost index ETFs. Start here before a taxable brokerage account.

3. Traditional IRA — if Roth not available

Pre-tax contributions may be deductible depending on your income and whether you have a workplace plan. Growth is tax-deferred. Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income. Same $7,000 limit as Roth (shared limit — total combined Roth + Traditional cannot exceed $7,000).

4. Taxable brokerage — for amounts beyond tax-advantaged limits

No contribution limits. Capital gains tax applies on profits — short-term gains (under 12 months) taxed as ordinary income; long-term gains (12+ months) at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income. Use after maximising tax-advantaged options.

US beginner priority in order: (1) Contribute enough to 401(k) to get full employer match. (2) Max Roth IRA ($7,000). (3) Continue 401(k) up to annual limit ($23,000 in 2024). (4) Taxable brokerage for any additional saving. Within each account: buy broad, low-cost index ETFs. VOO, VTI, VXUS, or the Fidelity zero-fee equivalents depending on your platform.

Key products and concepts that differ in Brazil

FGC guarantee: R$250,000 per CPF per institution

The Fundo Garantidor de Créditos protects CDB, LCI, LCA, poupança, and some other bank-issued products up to R$250,000 per CPF per institution. If you hold more than this in CDB with a single bank, consider spreading across multiple issuers to maintain full FGC coverage.

Income tax on fixed income: progressive, regressive by term

CDB and Tesouro Prefixado/IPCA+ pay income tax (IR) on returns: 22.5% for under 6 months, 20% for 6–12 months, 17.5% for 12–24 months, and 15% for over 24 months. LCI and LCA are income tax exempt for individuals — compare net return, not gross rate.

Come-cotas: the fund tax that runs on a schedule

Brazilian investment funds (fundos de investimento, not ETFs) pay come-cotas — a mandatory redemption of units to pay tax on 31 May and 30 November every year, even if you have not sold. This reduces your unit count while you hold. B3-listed ETFs like BOVA11 and IVVB11 do NOT use come-cotas — one reason many investors prefer ETFs over fundos.

B3 vs Tesouro Direto: same corretora, different systems

B3 is Brazil's stock and ETF exchange (stocks, ETFs, FIIs, options, BDRs). Tesouro Direto is a separate government bond programme operated by the Treasury. Both are accessible through your corretora, but they involve different risks, tax treatments, and market hours. Know which you are accessing before placing an order.

CDI rate: the benchmark for fixed income

Most Brazilian CDB products pay a percentage of the CDI (Certificado de Depósito Interbancário) rate, which closely tracks the Selic. At a Selic of ~14.75%, a CDB at 100% CDI pays approximately 14.75% per year before income tax. A CDB at 110% CDI pays more. Always compare net of tax when choosing between products.

BDRs: international shares on B3 in BRL

Brazilian Depositary Receipts let you hold certificates representing Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and hundreds of other global companies, denominated in BRL on B3. Currency risk is built in — when the BRL weakens against the USD, BDR values in BRL rise. This makes BDRs a natural FX hedge for Brazilian investors. Available at XP, Rico, BTG, and NuInvest.

8 things to compare regardless of country

1. Total cost

Brokerage, FX spread, account fees, inactivity fees, and fund management fees all matter together. A $0 commission trade can still be expensive if the FX spread is 0.7%.

2. Product access

Can you buy what you actually want? Check your specific ETFs, asset classes, account types (Roth IRA, Tesouro), and market access before opening an account.

3. Ownership model

CHESS (AU direct ownership), SIPC insurance (US custodian up to $500K), FGC (BR bank-issued products up to R$250K). Understand the protection structure before depositing.

4. Tax reporting

Annual statements, trade confirmations, distribution reports, and CSV exports. Test whether your accountant in your country can actually use the format before you have a problem at tax time.

5. Automation

If your plan is monthly DCA, auto-invest features and recurring deposits matter more than platform aesthetics. A platform that requires manual action each month creates a friction point that compounds over years.

6. Security

Two-factor authentication, withdrawal address controls, device management, and responsive support if something goes wrong. Check this before depositing anything meaningful.

7. Beginner safety

The best platform for a beginner is the one where it is hardest to accidentally buy the wrong thing. Fewer products, clearer screens, and deliberate friction in order placement are features, not flaws.

8. Support quality

Know how support works before you need it: phone, email, chat, response time, language availability. Platform problems happen at the worst times — know who to call.

How to avoid choosing the wrong platform

  1. Write your investing plan first. Platform choice should follow your goal, not create it. Know your asset type, country, frequency, and amount before looking at platforms.
  2. Choose your country and asset type. ASX ETF, US index fund, Brazilian Tesouro, or a mix? The right platform is dictated by where your first investment sits.
  3. Calculate the actual cost of your behaviour. Monthly $200 DCA and an occasional $5,000 buy have completely different fee sensitivities. Do the maths for your specific plan.
  4. Open with a small test amount. Learn deposits, order entry, confirmations, tax statements, and withdrawals before moving significant money in.
  5. Check tax reporting before it matters. Request a sample annual statement or check the help centre. The nicest-looking app becomes frustrating if export formats are incomplete at tax time.

Plainvest platform rule

A platform is a tool, not a strategy. Do not pick one because a creator promoted it, because the app looks exciting, or because it offers the most products. Pick the platform that makes your investing plan easier to follow, safer to record, and calmer to repeat — then use it consistently for years.
