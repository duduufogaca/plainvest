Australia Basics

Tax, CGT, and records without the panic.

This guide is educational only, not tax advice. It helps Australian beginners understand what usually creates tax questions, what records to keep, and what to ask a registered tax professional before tax time.

GoalKnow what to track before you invest.
Best timeBefore your first trade, not after June.
Key habitSave every statement and export.

The simple idea

In Australia, investing can create taxable events. The most common ones are dividends or ETF distributions, bank interest, capital gains when you sell an asset, and crypto disposals. You do not need to become a tax expert before investing, but you do need clean records and a basic understanding of the questions the ATO and your accountant may care about.

Plainvest rule: If money, units, crypto, or ownership changed hands, keep a record. Even if you do not know whether it is taxable yet, the record gives your tax professional something to work with.

Capital Gains Tax in plain English

Capital Gains Tax, or CGT, can apply when you dispose of an asset for more than its cost base. "Dispose" does not only mean selling for cash. It can also include gifting, swapping, or exchanging one asset for another.

What can trigger CGT?

Selling shares, ETFs, crypto, units, or property. Crypto-to-crypto swaps can also count as disposals because one asset was exchanged for another.

What is a capital gain?

Sale proceeds minus cost base. Cost base can include the purchase price plus some directly related costs such as brokerage.

12-month discount

Australian individuals may be eligible for a 50% CGT discount if they hold an asset for at least 12 months before disposal. Eligibility depends on the asset and your circumstances.

Capital losses

Losses can generally offset capital gains, but not ordinary income. You still need to keep records when something loses money.

ETF distributions are not always "just dividends"

Beginner investors often think an ETF distribution is simple cash income. In reality, a distribution statement can include Australian income, foreign income, franking credits, capital gains components, tax-deferred amounts, and other adjustments. Your broker may show cash received, but the annual tax statement explains what the payment was made of.

Crypto tax traps beginners miss

Crypto creates extra record-keeping pressure because platforms do not always provide tax-ready statements. A transfer between your own wallets may not be taxable, but a swap, sale, spend, staking reward, airdrop, or exchange conversion can create tax questions.

Swaps

BTC to ETH, ETH to stablecoin, or crypto to NFT can be treated like disposing of one asset and acquiring another.

Exchange rates

Keep AUD values at the time of each transaction. USD screenshots are not enough for Australian tax reporting.

Wallet transfers

Label transfers between your own wallets so they are not confused with sales or withdrawals.

Staking and rewards

Rewards may have income-tax and later CGT implications. Record date, value, and source.

Worked CGT example: selling shares after 12 months

Numbers are illustrative. Your actual outcome depends on your total taxable income, other gains or losses, and specific circumstances. This example helps you understand the calculation structure before speaking to a tax professional.

Purchase priceBought 50 units of an ETF at $40.00 per unit$2,000.00
Brokerage on purchaseAdded to cost base$9.50
Cost baseTotal cost of acquisition$2,009.50
Sale proceedsSold 50 units 14 months later at $58.00 per unit$2,900.00
Brokerage on saleSubtracted from proceeds– $9.50
Net proceedsAfter sale brokerage$2,890.50
Capital gain (before discount)Net proceeds minus cost base$881.00
50% CGT discount appliedHeld 14 months — discount eligible (check eligibility with accountant)– $440.50
Taxable capital gainAmount added to assessable income$440.50
Tax payable (example at 32.5% marginal rate)$440.50 × 32.5% — your rate depends on total income~$143
The discount in plain English: without the 12-month discount, $881 of gain would be taxed at your marginal rate (~$286 at 32.5%). By holding 14 months instead of 11, you halved the taxable gain — saving approximately $143. The exact saving depends on your marginal rate and total income. This is why the 12-month holding period is one of the most widely-used tax strategies in Australian investing.

Selling within 12 months: the difference

If the same trade above was closed after 8 months (not 14), the full $881 capital gain is assessable with no discount. At a 32.5% marginal rate, tax would be approximately $286 — twice the outcome of waiting 14 months. The only thing that changed was the holding period.

Records to keep

  • Broker contract notes and trade confirmations.
  • Dividend, ETF distribution, and annual tax statements.
  • CSV exports from brokers, crypto exchanges, and wallets.
  • Deposit, withdrawal, and transfer history.
  • Dates, quantities, prices, brokerage, FX fees, and exchange rates for non-AUD assets.
  • Notes about transfers between your own wallets or platforms.
  • Any accountant instructions or tax software reports used for lodgement.

A simple yearly workflow

  1. During the year: Save contract notes and export transactions every month or quarter.
  2. After 30 June: Download annual broker, ETF, dividend, bank interest, and crypto reports.
  3. Before lodging: Reconcile the number of units you hold with your transaction history.
  4. Ask questions early: If you sold, swapped crypto, received distributions, or moved assets, tell your accountant before they start.
  5. Keep it all: Store records in one folder by financial year.

Crypto tax software for Australians

If you have used a crypto exchange, made more than a handful of transactions, or swapped between assets, manual record-keeping can become complex quickly. Australian-built tax software can import your transaction history and produce reports your accountant can use.

What these tools do not replace

They categorise and calculate. They do not decide how to treat unusual situations, edge cases, staking income, NFTs, or airdrops. A registered tax agent still reviews and lodges.

Start before tax time

Setting up your crypto tax software at the start of the financial year is much easier than reconstructing a full year of transactions in June. Import once, then add exchanges as you go.

What to ask an accountant

Ask how CGT applies to your situation, how to handle ETF distributions and foreign income, whether the 12-month discount applies, how to record crypto activity, and what software or reports they prefer. Plainvest can help you prepare better questions, but a registered tax professional should handle personal tax advice.
