Choose a platform before choosing a button.

A beginner platform should be understandable, regulated where relevant, transparent on fees, and secure enough for the type of asset you want to buy.

Country and regulation

Check whether the platform operates legally in your country and what protections apply to your account or assets.

Product access

Confirm the platform offers what you need: ETFs, local shares, US shares, bonds, crypto, recurring buys, or retirement accounts.

Fees

Look for brokerage, spreads, FX conversion, management fees, deposit fees, withdrawal fees, and inactivity fees.

Support and education

Beginners need clear statements, tax documents, help center articles, and responsive support.

Security setup

  1. Use a strong unique password saved in a password manager.
  2. Enable two-factor authentication. Prefer authenticator apps or hardware keys over SMS when available.
  3. Verify withdrawal addresses and bank accounts carefully.
  4. Turn on notifications for logins, deposits, withdrawals, and trade confirmations.
  5. Never share login codes, seed phrases, or remote access to your screen.

Inside the platform: what to understand

  • Market order vs limit order, and why spread matters.
  • Settlement time, available cash, pending orders, and withdrawal rules.
  • Portfolio value vs invested amount vs unrealised gain or loss.
  • Dividend settings, reinvestment options, and tax documents.
  • Crypto deposits, network fees, withdrawal networks, and address formats if using crypto.
Premium support use: bring screenshots or questions to your Zoom call, but never share passwords, authentication codes, seed phrases, or private keys.

First transaction workflow

  1. Deposit a small amount first and confirm the platform records it correctly.
  2. Use a small test buy to understand order confirmation, price, spread, and fees.
  3. Download or locate the transaction statement inside the platform.
  4. If using crypto, test a tiny withdrawal before any larger transfer.
  5. Write down where tax records and yearly reports are located.

Questions to ask before trusting a platform

Who holds the asset?

Understand whether assets are held in your name, in custody, through a nominee structure, or inside a pooled platform arrangement.

What happens if the platform fails?

Look for client money rules, custody arrangements, insurance limits, and whether protections differ by asset type.

Can I leave easily?

Check withdrawal rules, transfer options, account closure, crypto withdrawal limits, and document export.

How does the platform make money?

Fees, spreads, FX margins, securities lending, premium plans, order flow, staking, or product commissions can change incentives.

Platform red flags

  • Guaranteed returns, urgent deadlines, or “risk-free” high yield.
  • No clear company registration, custody details, or fee schedule.
  • Pressure to deposit more before withdrawing.
  • Support asks for passwords, 2FA codes, private keys, or seed phrases.
  • Influencer-only credibility without regulated documentation.

Records and tax organisation

A good platform should make record keeping easy. Members should know where to find trade confirmations, dividend statements, deposit history, withdrawal history, FX conversion records, and yearly tax summaries.

  • Create a folder for annual statements and tax documents.
  • Record the original reason for opening the platform.
  • Keep a list of recurring deposits and recurring buys.
  • For crypto, record transaction hashes, networks, and wallet addresses used.
  • Review platform fees once per year because pricing can change.
