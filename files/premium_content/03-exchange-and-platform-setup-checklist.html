Support and education

Security setupConfiguração de segurança

  1. Use a strong unique password saved in a password manager.
  2. Enable two-factor authentication. Prefer authenticator apps or hardware keys over SMS when available.
  3. Verify withdrawal addresses and bank accounts carefully.
  4. Turn on notifications for logins, deposits, withdrawals, and trade confirmations.
  5. Never share login codes, seed phrases, or remote access to your screen.

First transaction workflowFluxo da primeira transação

  1. Deposit a small amount first and confirm the platform records it correctly.
  2. Use a small test buy to understand order confirmation, price, spread, and fees.
  3. Download or locate the transaction statement inside the platform.
  4. If using crypto, test a tiny withdrawal before any larger transfer.
  5. Write down where tax records and yearly reports are located.

Questions to ask before trusting a platformPerguntas a fazer antes de confiar em uma plataforma

Who holds the asset?

Understand whether assets are held in your name, in custody, through a nominee structure, or inside a pooled platform arrangement.

What happens if the platform fails?

Look for client money rules, custody arrangements, insurance limits, and whether protections differ by asset type.

Can I leave easily?

Check withdrawal rules, transfer options, account closure, crypto withdrawal limits, and document export.

How does the platform make money?

Fees, spreads, FX margins, securities lending, premium plans, order flow, staking, or product commissions can change incentives.

Multi-market platforms: accessing more than one country from one accountPlataformas multimercado: acessar mais de um país com uma única conta

Most beginners should start with a local platform and learn it well first. But some investors specifically need to hold assets across countries — for example, a Brazilian living in Australia, or an Australian wanting US ETFs alongside ASX shares. These notes explain when a global platform like Interactive Brokers makes sense.

Interactive Brokers: what makes it different

IBKR is one of the few brokers genuinely built for multi-currency, multi-market access from a single account. From one login, you can hold Australian shares, US ETFs, bonds, London-listed stocks, and assets in 30+ other countries. FX conversion is done at interbank rates plus ~0.002% — dramatically cheaper than the 0.5–1% most retail brokers charge. Available to residents of Australia, the US, Brazil, and most other countries. Regulated in each major jurisdiction (ASIC, SEC, FCA, etc.).

When IB makes sense vs. when it doesn't

IB makes sense when: you invest in multiple countries regularly; FX conversion costs matter at your investment size; you hold foreign bonds, futures, or assets not available on local platforms; you are an experienced investor who finds local platforms too limiting. IB does NOT make sense for: your first brokerage account; beginners who need simple trade confirmations; people investing only in one local market.

eToro: global social trading

Available in Australia, the US, and many other countries. Commission-free stock and ETF trades on many markets, with a 0.5% FX conversion fee on non-USD assets. Social trading features let you see what others hold and copy portfolios — which can be educational but also creates FOMO pressure. Not all features are available in all countries. Check country-specific regulatory status before opening.

The local platform rule

For most beginners, a local platform is the right first choice. It matches your local currency and tax reporting, has support in your language, and the regulation is familiar. Add a global platform only when you have a specific reason — not because it sounds more sophisticated. One platform used well beats three platforms used poorly.

Platform red flagsSinais de alerta em plataformas

  • Guaranteed returns, urgent deadlines, or “risk-free” high yield.
  • No clear company registration, custody details, or fee schedule.
  • Pressure to deposit more before withdrawing.
  • Support asks for passwords, 2FA codes, private keys, or seed phrases.
  • Influencer-only credibility without regulated documentation.

Records and tax organisationRegistros e organização fiscal

A good platform should make record keeping easy. Members should know where to find trade confirmations, dividend statements, deposit history, withdrawal history, FX conversion records, and yearly tax summaries.

  • Create a folder for annual statements and tax documents.
  • Record the original reason for opening the platform.
  • Keep a list of recurring deposits and recurring buys.
  • For crypto, record transaction hashes, networks, and wallet addresses used.
  • Review platform fees once per year because pricing can change.
