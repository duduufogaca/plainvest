Core investing terms

Asset

Something you own that may hold or grow value.

Portfolio

Your collection of investments.

Allocation

How your money is split between assets.

Diversification

Spreading money across assets to reduce dependence on one thing.

Risk tolerance

How much uncertainty and loss you can emotionally and financially handle.

Time horizon

How long you expect money to stay invested.

Compounding

Growth earning more growth over time.

Volatility

How sharply prices move up and down.

Drawdown

The fall from a previous high to a lower value.

Funds, shares, and income

ETF

A fund traded on an exchange like a share.

Index fund

A fund designed to track a market index.

Expense ratio

The yearly fund fee as a percentage.

Brokerage

The fee charged to place a trade.

Dividend

Cash paid by a company or fund to investors.

Distribution

A payment from a fund that can include income, gains, and tax components.

DRP

Dividend reinvestment plan: dividends buy more units instead of paying cash.

Yield

Income as a percentage of the investment price.

Liquidity

How easily something can be bought or sold.

Australian terms

ASX

Australian Securities Exchange.

CHESS

Australian share settlement system often connected with direct HIN ownership.

HIN

Holder Identification Number for CHESS-sponsored holdings.

Franking credit

A tax credit attached to some Australian dividends.

CGT

Capital Gains Tax rules that can apply when selling an asset for a gain.

Cost base

The amount used to calculate a capital gain or loss.

Superannuation

Australia's retirement investment system.

MySuper

A simple default super product for many workers.

Salary sacrifice

Pre-tax contributions to super through your employer.

Market and valuation terms

Bull market

A period where prices generally rise and optimism grows.

Bear market

A period where prices fall significantly and fear rises.

Correction

A meaningful decline, often described around 10% from recent highs.

Market cap

Company value based on share price times shares outstanding.

P/E ratio

Price compared with company earnings.

Moat

A durable business advantage.

Revenue

Money a company earns from sales.

Profit

Money left after expenses.

Cash flow

Cash moving in and out of a business.

Crypto terms

Bitcoin

A decentralized digital asset with a fixed supply schedule.

Exchange

A platform where users buy and sell crypto or investments.

Wallet

Software or hardware that controls crypto keys.

Seed phrase

The backup words that can restore access to a wallet.

Cold storage

Keeping private keys offline.

On-chain

Data visible on a public blockchain.

Halving

Bitcoin event where new issuance is cut in half.

Hash rate

Computing power securing proof-of-work networks.

Self-custody

Holding your own keys instead of relying on a platform.

United States investing terms

401(k)

Employer-sponsored retirement savings account. Contributions are pre-tax; withdrawals in retirement are taxed. Many employers match contributions — always contribute enough to get the full match.

Roth IRA

Individual retirement account funded with after-tax money. Growth and qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. 2024 contribution limit: $7,000 per year ($8,000 if over 50).

Traditional IRA

Retirement account where contributions may be tax-deductible. Growth is tax-deferred; withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.

ETF (US context)

Exchange-traded fund tracking an index (S&P 500, total market, bond market). SPY, IVV, VOO, VTI, and QQQ are among the most widely traded.

Expense ratio

Annual fee charged by a fund, expressed as a percentage. 0.03% means $3 per year on $10,000 invested. Compare expense ratios before choosing between similar ETFs.

Tax-loss harvesting

Selling an investment at a loss to offset capital gains, reducing tax. The proceeds are reinvested in a similar (but not identical) asset. Common in taxable accounts at year end.

Capital gains tax (US)

Short-term gains (held under 12 months) taxed at ordinary income rate. Long-term gains (held 12+ months) taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income. Very different from Australia's 50% discount model.

Brokerage account

A standard investment account outside of retirement wrappers. No contribution limits. Capital gains tax applies on profits. Dividends may also be taxable.

Index fund

A fund designed to track a market index. Can be a mutual fund (priced once daily) or an ETF (priced throughout the day). Typically lower cost than actively managed funds.

Brazilian investing terms

Tesouro Direto

Brazilian government's direct bond programme. Allows individuals to buy government bonds from R$30.

CDI

Interbank deposit rate in Brazil. Most fixed-income products pay a percentage of CDI.

Selic

Brazil's central bank benchmark interest rate, set by COPOM meetings.

CDB

Certificado de Depósito Bancário. A bank-issued fixed-income investment with FGC guarantee up to R$250k.

LCI / LCA

Bank bonds linked to real estate or agribusiness. Income tax exempt for individuals.

FII

Fundo de Investimento Imobiliário. Brazilian REIT traded on B3.

BDR

Brazilian Depositary Receipt. Certificate representing a foreign company share, traded in BRL on B3.

FGC

Fundo Garantidor de Créditos. Guarantees up to R$250,000 per CPF per institution for eligible products.

IPCA

Brazil's official consumer price inflation index, used in Tesouro IPCA+ bonds.

Beginner translation list

Fiat means government-issued money. Real return means return after inflation. Nominal return means return before inflation. Spread means the gap between buy and sell prices. Market order prioritises execution. Limit order sets your price. Rebalancing brings your portfolio back to target weights. Preservation age is when super access may begin, depending on rules. Concessional contribution is usually pre-tax super money. Non-concessional contribution is usually after-tax super money.
