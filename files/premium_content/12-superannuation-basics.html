Australia Basics

Your super is already investing.

Superannuation is often the largest investment Australians will ever own. This guide helps beginners understand what to check.

What super is

Super is a retirement account. Your employer contributes money, your fund invests it, and the account is generally locked until retirement age. Even if you have never bought an ETF yourself, your super means you are already an investor.

What to compare

Fees

Admin fees, investment fees, insurance premiums, and indirect costs can quietly reduce long-term returns.

Investment option

MySuper default, balanced, growth, high growth, indexed, ethical, or DIY-style options can behave very differently.

Performance

Compare long-term returns after fees, not only the last 12 months.

Insurance

Check whether default insurance is useful, duplicated, too expensive, or not needed for your situation.

Beginner checklist

  • Find all your super accounts and consider consolidation if appropriate.
  • Know your current fund, balance, fees, insurance, and investment option.
  • Compare your fund using official comparison tools and fund documents.
  • Understand concessional vs non-concessional contribution basics before adding extra money.
  • Ask a professional if you are unsure about tax, caps, or personal suitability.

Plain-English warning

Super is powerful because it compounds for decades, but it is also personal. Tax, contribution caps, age, income, insurance, and retirement goals matter. Learn the basics here, then get qualified advice before making big super decisions.
