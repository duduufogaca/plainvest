Why ETFs are where most beginners start

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) bundles many investments — sometimes hundreds or thousands of companies — into one. Buy a single broad ETF and you instantly own a slice of the whole market. That built-in diversification, low cost, and simplicity is why ETFs are the most common starting point for new investors.

💡 Instead of guessing which company will win, a broad ETF lets you own all of them. You capture the market's long-term growth without picking individual stocks.

What makes ETFs beginner-friendly

  • Diversification — one purchase spreads risk across many companies.
  • Low cost — broad index ETFs typically have very low fees.
  • Simplicity — no need to research and track dozens of individual stocks.

Pair ETFs with consistency

ETFs work best with a steady habit — investing a fixed amount regularly (dollar-cost averaging) and holding for the long term. The Plainvest learning path shows you exactly how to choose and set this up as a beginner.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ETF?

An exchange-traded fund holds a basket of many assets — often hundreds of companies — in a single investment, giving instant diversification at low cost.

Why do beginners start with ETFs?

A single broad ETF spreads money across many companies automatically, lowering single-company risk while keeping costs low. You own all of them instead of picking winners.

What return do ETFs provide?

Broad market ETFs have historically averaged ~7–8% per year before inflation over long periods, though returns vary. These are educational assumptions, not guarantees.
